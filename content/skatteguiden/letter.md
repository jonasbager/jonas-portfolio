## How I think about AI in IT

The best IT department is the one people barely notice, and the second best is the one they actually like calling. AI should make both easier, not replace the people doing it.

In practice that means agents take the repetitive work - provisioning, licence clean-up, the fifth password reset of the week - so the humans in IT have time for the conversations that matter. A person still owns every access decision. And every agent gets treated like a new employee: an identity, the access it needs and nothing more, and an offboarding date.

Too often, IT is the department people dread: the one that says no, blocks initiatives and treats every request as a risk. I want to build the opposite. A small, quick and modern IT function that starts from yes, sees requests as opportunities and gives every colleague the best tools to do their best work.

That doesn't mean going soft on security. Threats are getting smarter, and attackers use AI too, so IT should use the same tools, only better, to stay ahead. Part of the job is also telling the hype from what actually works, and being conscious about what is implemented and what is not.
